A person counts. The camera does not mark its own homework.

A person counts a random sample of windows. We compare the two counts and keep the clip for every row. We report the sample size with every result, so you can see how much weight it carries.

39 datasets. None of them ours. None of them failed.

We built a model from 40 images on each of 39 public datasets we did not choose. The pass mark was set before we ran: under 55% of a fully labelled model counts as a failure. Zero of 39 hit it, with no per-domain tuning. The median was 89% of a fully hand-labelled model, and for each site we pick the 40.

On frames with none of the target objects, the single 40-image model made 11.5 false detections per 100 frames, against 104.8 for the three separate single-object models it replaces, roughly 9 times fewer.

Photos instead of counts.

In a pilot with one of the world's largest telecom tower companies, the same platform checked photos for bolt and rust faults on 74 towers it had never seen. It flagged 42 of 45 photos with a fault and cleared 33 of 41 clean photos. This is a pilot, not a finished product.

What we do not claim.

  • We do not claim a final error bar for any Healthfields use case. The deployment is ongoing.
  • We do not claim results in industries where we have no customer. Other industries on this site are examples of where a checkpoint exists.
  • Protective-gear checks are a labelled demonstration, not a measured result.
