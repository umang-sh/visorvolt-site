// The Proof

39 datasets. None of them ours.
None of them failed.

Assets first, then damage. Every box is a real detection on a held-out frame.

Assets

Pass mark set before we ran: under 55% of a fully-labeled model counts as a failure. Zero of 39 hit it — no per-domain tuning, no architecture changes. Bulk goods is a separate pooled public benchmark, measured the same way.

Each score is against a model trained on that task’s entire labeled dataset. Median 87%, none below 55%. Single tiles carry ±3–5 points of seed-draw variance; the median is stable. Harder problems land nearer 87%, well-conditioned ones nearer 90% — we tell you which end you are in before you buy. One benchmark (underwater-objects) is withheld: its comparison model was too weak to divide by.

Defects

Finding the asset is the easy half; the value is in the damage — rare, subjective, and expensive for an expert to box. Same recipe, unchanged.

Four industries, one system, nothing rebuilt between them. The cable break at 60% is the weakest and it is here on purpose — thin, low-contrast damage on a busy background is the hardest thing we do, and you should see it before you buy. ±3–5 points of seed-draw variance per tile. The electronics tile is a separate PCB benchmark whose supervised ceiling is unusually high (AP50 0.98) — a strong result on an easy dataset.
