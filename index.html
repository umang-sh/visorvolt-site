Checkpoints

A checkpoint is any place where something has to be counted, logged or checked.

Pick the place. We build the count or the check for it, from your own footage. The examples below are different checkpoints, not different products.

Vehicle gate

The truck is read by number plate and stamped with the time. A car is logged as a car.

Where it shows up: factory gates, quarries, construction sites, ports, cold stores, campuses.

Dock

When a truck docks and when it leaves the dock, with the time of each.

Where it shows up: any site with loading bays.

Gate to gate

One visit from the plate read on the way in to the plate read on the way out. In the clip: about 2 hours 16 minutes.

People crossing a line

Each person who crosses is detected and logged with the time.

Where it shows up: entrances, restricted areas, shift changes.

Flows inside the site

A loaded pallet followed from the dock to the store.

Where it shows up: factories, warehouses, cold stores, any place goods change rooms.

Production line

A running count of cartons at the packing line.

Where it shows up: any line where finished goods leave one point.

Dispatch

Cartons counted live as they are loaded onto the truck.

How it works

Built for your site, then audited.

  1. Show us the checkpoint.

    Send a few minutes of footage from one camera, or tell us what you want counted or checked.

  2. We build it for your site.

    We pick about 40 images from your own footage and label them ourselves. There is no labelling project on your side.

  3. It runs on your site.

    Counting and review happen on a computer on your site, with no internet needed. Every row is kept with its clip.

  4. A person audits it, and it improves every month.

    A person counts a random sample and compares it with the camera. Misses feed the next version, and a person approves every change.

Research

39 datasets. None of them ours. None of them failed.

We built a model from 40 images on each of 39 public datasets we did not choose. The pass mark was set before we ran: under 55% of a fully labelled model counts as a failure. Zero of 39 hit it, with no per-domain tuning. The median was 89% of a fully hand-labelled model. For each site we pick the 40, so you do not run a labelling project.

On frames with none of the target objects, the single 40-image model made 11.5 false detections per 100 frames, against 104.8 for the three separate single-object models it replaces, roughly 9 times fewer.
